# Asset Accounting

## 📘 Overview

Asset Accounting (FI-AA) manages the financial lifecycle of a company's fixed assets.

For Nikhil Manufacturing Pvt. Ltd., Asset Accounting covers asset acquisition, capitalization, depreciation, transfer and retirement.

## 🔄 Asset Lifecycle

**Asset Acquisition → Capitalization → Depreciation → Transfer / Adjustment → Retirement**

The asset lifecycle records the financial impact of an asset throughout its useful life.

---

## 1. Asset Master

An asset master record contains important information about an individual fixed asset.

Examples include:

- Asset description
- Asset class
- Cost center
- Capitalization date
- Useful life
- Depreciation method
- Depreciation area

---

## 2. Asset Acquisition

When the company purchases a fixed asset, the asset is capitalized.

### Example

Nikhil Manufacturing Pvt. Ltd. purchases machinery for INR 500,000.

A simplified accounting impact may be:

**Debit:** Machinery / Asset – INR 500,000  
**Credit:** Vendor / Bank – INR 500,000

The exact posting depends on the acquisition process and configuration.

---

## 3. Depreciation

Depreciation allocates the depreciable cost of an asset over its useful life.

### Example

Assume machinery costing INR 500,000 has a useful life of 5 years and straight-line depreciation is used for learning purposes.

Annual depreciation:

**INR 500,000 ÷ 5 = INR 100,000**

A simplified depreciation posting would be:

**Debit:** Depreciation Expense – INR 100,000  
**Credit:** Accumulated Depreciation – INR 100,000

---

## 4. Asset Transfer

An asset may be transferred between organizational assignments when business requirements change.

Examples include:

- Transfer between cost centers
- Transfer between locations
- Transfer between organizational units

The accounting treatment depends on the type of transfer and system configuration.

---

## 5. Asset Retirement

An asset may be retired when it is:

- Sold
- Scrapped
- No longer usable

When an asset is retired, its acquisition value and accumulated depreciation are considered in determining the resulting accounting impact.

---

## 🔗 Integration with General Ledger

Asset Accounting is integrated with the General Ledger.

Typical flow:

**Asset Transaction → Asset Accounting → G/L → Financial Reporting**

Asset transactions can update relevant asset, depreciation and expense accounts.
